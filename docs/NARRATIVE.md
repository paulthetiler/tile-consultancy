# Customer-facing narrative — the plain version

**This document governs everything a customer reads.** Where it conflicts with the strategy in `DISCOVERY.md` or `DISCOVERY-02-forensic.md`, this wins for customer-facing copy.

The two discovery documents remain useful — but as **internal rationale only** (the visual system, information architecture, SEO plan, component inventory, roadmap, report/library governance). Their *brand-philosophy* language — "Bureau of Record," "you become the file the authority is drawn from," the Building → Instrument → Examination → Record arc — is **internal, never on the site.**

**What we keep from the forensic direction:** the visual language (specimen plates, calibration wedge, measurement annotation, monospace data, the single red used only for a measured finding) and the evidence-first approach (every claim measured, referenced to a standard, documented). We *show* these. We don't explain them.

**The 10-second test:** a first-time visitor should be able to say, out loud, what we do within ten seconds. If a line needs explaining, cut it. If a concept needs a metaphor, it's too clever.

---

## What we are — one line

> Independent commercial tiling consultants. We investigate failures, inspect installations, produce authoritative reports, and specify the remedial work.

## The hero (homepage, first screen)

- **Headline:** *We investigate, inspect and report on commercial tiling.*
- **Sub-line:** *When a commercial tiling installation fails or needs checking, we find out what's wrong, prove it, and set out the fix — independently, with the evidence behind every finding.*

That is the whole proposition. Everything below it is detail and proof.

## The four things we do (plain)

| | Service | Plain description | Covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Investigate** | Find out why a tiling installation failed. | Defect investigations · expert witness |
| 02 | **Inspect** | Check whether an installation is right. | Inspections · condition surveys · progress & QA |
| 03 | **Report** | Produce authoritative technical reports, backed by evidence. | Technical reporting |
| 04 | **Recommend** | Set out exactly what to put right, and how. | Remedial specifications |

## Homepage order (simplified)

1. **Hero** — the one-line proposition above.
2. **What we do** — the four services, one plain sentence each.
3. **The evidence** — one real annotated finding: a defect, measured, checked against the standard, pass/fail. Proof, not adjectives.
4. **Reports** — a look at the actual deliverable; the report is the product.
5. **Who we help & who we are** — the clients we work for, and the named person behind the reports.
6. **Contact** — a quiet, plain way to instruct us.

## Independence & evidence (say it plainly, once)

> We install nothing and hold no allegiance. Every finding is measured, referenced to a standard, and documented — so it stands up in a handover meeting or a tribunal.

That single sentence carries the whole "evidence-first / independent" position. No further philosophy required.

## What not to say

- No metaphors that need unpacking (no "bureau of record," no "the record").
- Don't *claim* authority ("the leading / definitive authority"). Show the evidence and let the reader conclude it.
- Banned words: *world-class, bespoke, solutions, passionate, cutting-edge, seamless, trusted, unrivalled.* Name the standard instead of reaching for the adjective.
- Don't invent a second word for a thing that already has a plain one. "Report" not "instrument of record." "Finding" not "specimen" (in customer prose — "specimen ref" is fine *inside* a technical plate, where it reads as authentic labelling, not jargon to decode).

## The knowledge library (plain framing)

Not "the industry's definitive resource" (that's an internal ambition). To a customer it is simply: *a growing library of the technical facts this work turns on — defect types, tolerances, standards, test methods — built from real casework.* We let its usefulness earn the reputation; we don't announce it.
