# Discovery — Phase 1, Round 2

**The forensic turn: evidence, reports-as-product, and the definitive reference**

**Supersedes / extends:** `docs/DISCOVERY.md` (Round 1). Where the two differ, this document wins.
**Method:** produced through a multi-perspective process — six independent naming strategies scored by a three-lens panel and then attacked by adversarial refuters, plus four parallel specialist tracks (positioning, reports, knowledge library, visual system). The naming recommendation below is one the panel reached *and then had knocked down* on load-bearing grounds; the version that survived the attack is what's recommended. Two agents (a practical/SEO refuter and a completeness critic) were lost to a session limit; their angles are partly covered by the scalability refuter (which hit trademark/ownability directly) and by the synthesis critique in §10.

---

## 0. The shift in one line

**From CLAIMED AUTHORITY to DEMONSTRATED EVIDENCE.**

Round 1 positioned the firm as *"the independent authority."* But authority you have to *assert* is authority you don't yet have. The forensic turn inverts it: stop claiming the last word and become **the party that establishes the facts the last word is drawn from.** You never call yourself the authority — you become the file the authority is drawn from.

The visual metaphor makes the same move, in four stages:

> **BUILDING → INSTRUMENT → EXAMINATION → RECORD**

- **Building** (rejected in Round 1): glossy architecture. The building is never the subject; at most it is *the scene*.
- **Instrument** (Round 1): the calibrated measuring tool as the unifying idea. Correct — but it stops at the tool. A photo of a calliper is still a product shot.
- **Examination** (this round): the tool *in use against a specimen* — raking light finding a lipped edge, calliper jaws closing on a defect. The subject becomes the *act of measuring*; the tile becomes a *specimen*.
- **Record** (where the brand lands): the site is not a brochure that *contains* evidence — the site **is** an evidence record. The mental model is the forensic pathology atlas and the deposition exhibit bundle: every image a numbered, scaled, dated specimen plate; every report Exhibit A; the whole thing reading like a case file assembled by someone whose duty is to the court.

The commercial payoff is exact: **a case file cannot look like it is selling.** Restraint stops being a taste borrowed from Apple and becomes a *genre convention* borrowed from forensic science — which is a far stronger justification, and one no competitor is disciplined enough to hold.

## 1. The naming decision — challenged, and resolved

You asked me to challenge the surname harder against a *scalable, multi-inspector* firm. I ran six naming strategies (including a genuine steelman of the surname, so it wasn't strawmanned) past a three-lens panel, then had the winner attacked. Here is what came back.

### 1.1 You are right to reject the surname — and here's the rigorous reason

A surname brand scores highest on exactly one axis: **personal credibility**. But that credibility is *already delivered* by the named author's signature on every report (see §1.4) — so the firm's name doesn't need to carry it. Meanwhile the surname is penalised hard everywhere that matters for scale:

- **Founder ceiling** — the name never *creates* scale; it caps the firm at one reputation.
- **Key-person concentration** — illness, conflict-out on a case, or departure of the founder dents the *whole* brand.
- **Reputational contagion** — one bad day for the named person is a bad day for the institution.
- **Succession & exit friction** — a personal-name asset *depreciates* at succession and sells poorly.

The profession proves surname firms *can* scale (Sandberg, Hollis). But "can, despite the name" is not "because of it." For the firm you're describing — many inspectors, an appreciating institutional asset, a saleable business — an **impersonal, institutional mark is correct.** Verdict upheld.

### 1.2 The obvious replacement is a trap

All three judges independently reached the same specific answer: an institutional **metrology word**, with **"Datum"** as the lead (a datum is the fixed reference every party triangulates from — it takes no side; it ladders into *the Datum Report* and *the Datum Index*). It's a seductive, on-strategy answer.

**Then both adversarial refuters knocked it down** — and they're right:

- **Ownability (the load-bearing failure).** "Datum" is a generic geodesy/engineering term already worn by dozens of live consultancies (Datum Group, Datum Consulting, Datum Technologies…) — effectively **unregistrable** as a distinctive mark in the relevant classes. "Vernier" collides head-on with **Vernier Science Education**, an established measurement-instrument brand — a direct conflict in the *adjacent metrology space*. A branded house whose entire equity strategy is *"everything welds to one mark"* is **maximally exposed if that mark is legally weak.** As the firm expands into new territories (EUIPO), adjacent forensics (screeds, waterproofing, façades — each a new class), and public expert-witness prominence (where the name is read aloud in court and Googled), an unownable mark invites collision and can't be defended against a copycat.
- **The endpoint is worse than a surname rebrand.** By the time you're forced to change it, a numbered, *citable* report archive (`Report 2426-C`, sitting in filed litigation bundles and referenced in later judgments) and the knowledge library are permanently fused to a name you can't retroactively re-mark on historical legal documents. **You'd be rebranding a body of evidence, not just a logo.**

So the naive move — grab a nice dictionary word like *Datum* — fails the exact scalability test you asked me to apply. This is the single most important finding of the round.

### 1.3 The recommendation that survived the attack

**Adopt an impersonal, institutional brand — but the mark must be a genuinely coined, distinctive, *registrable* neologism, and clearance is the GATE, not a footnote.**

- **Coined, not dictionary.** A measurement/precision *feel* (Latinate, instrument-adjacent), but an **invented, ownable token** — so goodwill can safely compound into it for two decades. (Of the panel's candidates, only the coined *Metria*-class option plausibly survives clearance — offered as an illustration of the *class*, not a final name. *Kilter* is struck outright: "off-kilter" literally means *out of true / out of alignment* — a self-inflicted gift to cross-examining counsel for a firm whose whole reputation is proving tolerance.)
- **Registrability as a precondition.** Clear the mark as registrable and enforceable across every Nice class the firm will ever touch — **42** (technical/testing/surveying), **35** (consultancy), **41** (the published library), **45** (expert-witness/legal-adjacent) — and every territory it may plausibly enter (**UK IPO + EUIPO** minimum), with matching `.com`/`.co.uk` and social handles secured, **before one pound of goodwill accrues.** Treat this as a short clearance sprint with a trademark attorney at the *start* of brand design.
- **A descriptor lockup does the searchable work.** The coined mark carries premium/ownership; a plain descriptor beneath it — *"Commercial tiling & surface-systems consultants"* / *"the record on commercial tiling"* — carries clarity and SEO. Same pattern recommended in Round 1, now load-bearing.

Under that discipline, the branded-house architecture in §1.4 is *the most scalable structure on the table* — more scalable than any surname. The refuter's own words: *"the fix is not to abandon the approach but to prune the shortlist and reorder the process."*

> **Honest tradeoff for you to own:** a coined mark is safe, ownable and scalable but has to *earn* its meaning over time; a dictionary word (*Datum*) is instantly meaningful but unownable. The adversarial finding tilts hard toward coined. I recommend coined. But this is a genuine fork and it's yours to call — with eyes open.

### 1.4 The architecture that resolves surname-vs-scale (independent of the exact name)

A **branded house** with a **dual layer** — the model is a **barristers' chambers**: an institution whose reputation scales, inside which every practitioner is individually named, personally accountable, and bound by an overriding duty to the court.

- **The FIRM is the bureau / lab:** the mark, the house method, the standards library, the calibrated instruments, and an *independent peer-review* countersignature. Goodwill compounds *here*.
- **The PERSON is the witness:** CPR Part 35 / PD35 require a *named individual* to author the report and sign a personal statement of truth — the duty to the court is personal and cross-examinable; you cannot file a report authored by "[Firm] Ltd." So every report names and CVs its author and carries their *personally signed* declaration of independence.
- **Signing convention:** *"Prepared by A. N. Expert BEng CEng MICE — [Firm]. Technical peer review: [second inspector]."*
- **New inspectors join as named authors under one trusted mark** — no rebrand, no personal-name equity created or destroyed when a founder retires or is conflicted out. Goodwill accrues to the *mark, the report format and the library*, so a departing star dents but does not define the brand.

This is the concrete answer to your challenge: **the institution carries the mark; the individuals carry the accountability.** You get institutional scale *and* personal Part 35 credibility without trading one for the other.

### 1.5 Two credibility guardrails the adversarial pass forced (do not skip)

The second refuter found a self-inflicted wound in the *marketing*, not the name:

1. **Ring-fence the word "independent."** Do **not** market a same-firm second-inspector check as an *"independent review"* — that reviewer is on the firm's payroll and shares the fee. A barrister will place *"independently checked"* directly beside the expert's own sworn declaration of independence and detonate it. Badge the internal check **"technical peer review" / "QA review"** everywhere — signature block *and* public copy. Reserve *"independent"* strictly for (a) the expert's personal Part 35 declaration and (b) the firm's *structural* independence from the parties.
2. **Keep the Part 35 personal core dominant over the product livery.** Productising the report is right — but the numbering, QA mark and calibration page must be *subordinate* to the named author, CV, statement of truth and signed declaration, and must be *substantively real* (an actual instrument-traceability record), never decorative sales livery. Otherwise opposing counsel runs *"this is a [Firm] sales product, engineered to be sold, not the personal opinion you owe the court."* Evidence genuine structural independence too: **no contingency/outcome-linked fees, and never also trade as a tiling contractor.**

## 2. Positioning — The Bureau of Record

**"The Instrument" is demoted, not discarded.** It was the wrong *organising* metaphor but the right *sensory* one, and separating those two jobs is the fix:

- **As sensory metaphor it survives intact** — the graticule grid, the single red index line, monospace data, calliper-and-laser photography, mechanical motion. This is the most ownable, least-copyable texture in the category. Keep every bit of it. It becomes **the fingerprint of the *method*, not the identity of the *firm*.**
- **As organising metaphor it fails** — an instrument is a single passive tool that produces a *reading*, not a *finding*; it can't scale to a practice or an archive, and pushed hard it becomes exactly the cold, inhuman gimmickry the brief warns against.

**The organising metaphor moves up a level, from the tool to the institution that wields it: the BUREAU OF RECORD** — a standing evidentiary body. It holds against all four new pressures where "instrument" breaks: a bureau *establishes and holds what is true* (evidence is its native output); it *issues the record* (the report becomes the artefact the institution exists to produce, not a deliverable); it *maintains the reference* (the library is the reference section, not marketing); and it is an *institution, not a person* (grows by adding named inspectors).

> Rejected: **"The Laboratory" / "forensic laboratory" as the identity.** It over-claims — "laboratory" invites the fair question of what UKAS / ISO 17025 accreditation backs it, an over-claim that is *fatal in exactly the courtroom that matters most*. It also mis-describes the work (the core is on-site inspection, not benchtop testing) and drags fastest toward CSI kitsch. Own forensic *rigour* as a property; don't take "laboratory" as the noun of the brand.

### Positioning statement (public-facing)

> The independent bureau of record for commercial tiling and hard-surface finishes. We measure the defect, establish the mechanism of failure, and issue the report the industry treats as evidence. We install nothing and hold no allegiance — the finding is the only product, and it is built to stand on its own: in a handover meeting, or under cross-examination.

## 3. Voice & lexicon — the evidential register

Evolve the voice from *"well-written engineer's report"* to **evidential register** — the grammar of a witness statement fused with a lab note.

- **Evidence-chain sentence structure.** Order every technical claim **observation → measurement → standard → finding**: *"Lippage at the threshold measured 3.1 mm. BS 5385-3 permits 2 mm. The finding is a non-conformity."* The structure itself is the rigour.
- **Let the number carry the affect.** Never editorialise (*serious, shocking, poor workmanship*); quantify and let the reader feel the alarm. Characterisation is the mark of a partisan; quantification is the mark of a witness.
- **Provenance on every claim** — instrument, standard, tolerance, date attached to the assertion. Tone as chain of custody.
- **Two registers, cleanly separated.** *Institutional* voice for the site (calm, third-person, restrained). *Signatory* voice for reports and library entries (first-person, named, personally accountable — *"I inspected…", "in my opinion, on the balance of probabilities…"*). Keeping them distinct is what makes the reports read as real documents and the site read as an institution.
- **Vocabulary to own:** *the record / on the record / issue the record · findings (never "opinions") · measured, not asserted · tolerance / out of tolerance · chain of custody · on the balance of probabilities · instructed / instruction · mechanism of failure · as-built vs as-specified · specimen · datum · calibrated · lippage; deflection (L/360); moisture content (%WME); surface regularity (SR) · non-conformity · the reference · duty to the court (CPR Part 35) · stands up under cross-examination.*
- **Banned lexicon, enforced:** *passionate, world-class, bespoke, solutions, cutting-edge, trusted, leading, seamless, unrivalled, "we're proud to."* Name the standard instead of reaching for the adjective.
- **Jargon guardrail:** every technical page carries one plain-language throughline a non-technical buyer (developer, FM) can follow. Specificity is texture, not a wall.

**Lead tagline:** *"We take measurements, not sides."* (Runners-up: *"Findings, not opinions." · "Measured, not asserted." · "The record on commercial tiling."*)

## 4. Reports as a hero product

**The report is the product — so sell it the way Leica sells a lens: as a precision instrument you can inspect before you commit.** Reports stop being downloadable afterthoughts and become the site's central exhibit.

### 4.1 The specimen — the atomic unit of the whole system

The organising atom is the **specimen**: one measured piece of evidence rendered as a self-contained annotated object. A report is then a structured *assembly of specimens plus reasoning*. Fields:

`specimen ref (SP-07)` · forensic macro photograph · measurement overlay (value + dimension lines + red index line) · location metadata (grid ref, room, datum) · **measured value vs permissible tolerance → PASS/FAIL** · cited standard + clause · one-line diagnosis · severity chip · **provenance chip**.

Authored **once** (as typed data / MDX) and reused **everywhere** — inside reports, in the evidence viewer, as illustration in the library, on the homepage, and in the printed PDF. It is simultaneously **proof, teaching object and SEO object** (each specimen gets a canonical URL and can be sent to a solicitor as a link). Crucially, it is also the unit that makes both **confidentiality-safe showcasing** and **multi-inspector scale** possible: the transferable authority is the *house specimen format*, not any one principal.

### 4.2 Report anatomy (the exemplar the site shows)

Control sheet · Instruction & issues · Basis, access & limitations · Methodology & instrumentation · Standards & benchmark documents · Findings & observations (the specimens) · Schedule of defects (sortable table) · Analysis & causation · Conclusions · Remedial specification · **Declarations & compliance (CPR Part 35 statement of truth)** · Appendices & evidence record (calibration certificates, photographic schedule, author CV, chain of custody). *Document-control discipline is the first credibility signal — it says this is a controlled instrument, not a letter.*

### 4.3 The report family (products)

Inspection Note · Condition Survey Report · Progress / QA Inspection Report · **Defect Investigation Report** (core commercial product) · **Expert Report — CPR Part 35** (the flagship; the report the whole aesthetic exists to make believable) · Report Review / Second Opinion (positions the firm as the arbiter of others' work) · Remedial Specification.

### 4.4 On-site experience

- **Specimen viewer** — a deep-zoom, pannable forensic macro with **toggleable annotation layers**: raw photograph ↔ measurement overlay ↔ standard call-out ↔ a tolerance readout that resolves to **PASS/FAIL**. Operating it feels like reading an instrument display, not scrolling a gallery. Keyboard-operable, reduced-motion-safe.
- **Annotated report reader** — a browsable exemplar report, paged section-by-section against the anatomy, restrained document chrome, the craft on display.
- **Case specimens** — anonymised cases assembled from specimen objects (instruction → specimens → finding → outcome), structured and low-narrative.
- **Sample download** — a fully-designed exemplar PDF, itself a brand surface built from the same tokens; the highest tier sits behind a quiet *"request the specimen report"* line (lead capture without a salesy gate).

### 4.5 Confidentiality — provenance is a feature, not a risk

**Real client reports are never published.** Three sanctioned provenance classes, each *honestly labelled* with a mono chip so provenance is never ambiguous:

1. **SYNTHETIC EXEMPLAR** — purpose-built, technically accurate but wholly fabricated for the site; no real project, so full fidelity at zero confidentiality risk. **Labelled as illustrative, never passed off as a genuine case record.** The flagship showcase is built on these.
2. **ANONYMISED CASE** — real settled matters only, stripped of every identifier, location generalised (*"a North-West station concourse, 2021"*), consent where borderline.
3. **REDACTED EXTRACT** — genuine pages with designed redaction bars over confidential fields; the redaction is styled as part of the forensic aesthetic and itself signals discretion.

Publishing gate (all three must pass): *no identifiable party · not sub judice / no privilege issue · consent obtained OR fully synthetic.* Expert-witness material **defaults to synthetic exemplars** because privilege and court permission make real extracts hazardous. Being transparent about provenance is itself a credibility signal — and it is the discipline that keeps the showcase honest.

### 4.6 Hero treatment & a navigation change

The homepage **Evidence** movement is upgraded from a passive case excerpt to a **single live hero specimen**: on load, the signature motion draws the red index line to a lippage measurement and the tolerance readout resolves to **FAIL** — the "instrument settling" gesture, used once — with a quiet line, *"The report is the product. Examine one,"* linking into Reports.

**Reports is promoted to a top-level nav destination** (a deliberate change from Round 1's footer placement, justified by the hero-product direction). Recommended primary nav: **Services · Approach · Reports · Expertise · The Reference · Contact** — with **Sectors** demoted to in-context/footer to hold the calm header.

## 5. The definitive knowledge library — "The Reference"

Planned from day one as a **controlled reference work, not a blog.** The unit is the **canonical, versioned, citable ENTRY** — a defect, a tolerance, a standard clause, a test method — never the chronological "post." Four properties make it *definitive* rather than editorial:

1. **Organised for look-up, not reading-latest** — reach any fact from multiple orthogonal axes (defect × standard × material × environment × method), like a clinical atlas or a legal reporter, not a feed.
2. **Maintained against live standards** — every entry dated, version-numbered, with a *last-reviewed* stamp, a named reviewer, and a link to the BS/EN documents it depends on, so it flags for revision when they change.
3. **Evidence-backed by proprietary field data** — each entry links to real, redacted, measured material from actual investigations. A content marketer cannot fabricate this.
4. **Built to be cited** — stable entry IDs and permanent *"cite this"* references, used first in the firm's own Part 35 reports, and over time by other experts, solicitors and manufacturers.

> The definitive move: commercial-tiling failure modes are a **finite, enumerable field.** Own the *complete enumeration* at reference-work discipline from entry one, and the framework reads as authoritative even while coverage is still partial. *"A blog answers 'what's new'; this answers 'what is true, and by what authority.'"*

### 5.1 Naming the library

- **Umbrella (lead):** **"The Commercial Tiling Reference"** — descriptive, institutional, SEO-perfect; it literally claims the definitive slot and owns the exact-match head term; belongs to the practice, not a person.
- **Flagship spine:** **"The Defect Atlas"** — borrows the authority of clinical atlases (Radiopaedia, DermNet); the single most linkable/citable asset; the name that gets cited in reports.
- (If the brand mark itself is coinable and clearable, a house sub-brand — e.g. *"[Mark] Index"* — can sit over the umbrella; but the descriptive umbrella carries the SEO.)

### 5.2 Taxonomy (also the SEO architecture — hub-and-spoke)

Defect Atlas (flagship) · Standards & Codes · **Tolerances & Datums** · Materials & Systems · Test & Measurement Methods · Substrates & Backgrounds · Environments & Applications · Investigation & Evidence · Casework (redacted) · Nomenclature (glossary).

### 5.3 Content types

Defect Entry (atlas monograph, identical structure every time) · Standard Explainer / Clause Commentary · **Tolerance Reference (data table)** · Method Note · Materials/System Note · Redacted Case Record · Diagnostic Flow / Decision Aid · Glossary Term · Instrument/Test-equipment Note · Annotated Forensic Plate.

### 5.4 Governance (what makes it a reference, not content)

Every entry is a **controlled document**: unique stable ID, version number, first-published + last-reviewed + next-review dates, **named author + named reviewer**, published on a two-eyes-minimum lifecycle (draft → technical review against the primary standard → editorial/voice review → publish → periodic revision). A **standards-watch register** maps each entry to the BS/EN documents it depends on, so a revision to (say) BS 5385 auto-flags every dependent entry — the maintenance engine a rival can't cheaply match. **Errata are logged transparently like a journal, never silently edited.** The library publishes its own **masthead / editorial statement** declaring the review process, independence and correction policy — the reference's equivalent of the CPR Part 35 independence statement, and a direct E-E-A-T signal.

### 5.5 The multi-inspector contribution model (the scalability answer, made tangible)

Authority is *engineered to live in the institution and its review process, not in one person* — which is exactly why a multi-inspector reference is worth **more** than a principal's byline. Roles: Contributor · Technical Reviewer · Editor · Editor-in-Chief. Every entry shows *"Authored by X, reviewed by Y"* under one masthead. **The pipeline is the day-job:** every real inspection contributes redacted evidence, measured plates and case records to the corpus — so **headcount growth = corpus growth at near-zero marginal content cost.** A new inspector inherits the whole corpus's credibility on day one; the corpus inherits their fresh evidence. This converts the brand from a *depreciating personal asset* into an *appreciating, transferable institutional one* that supports partners, associates and eventual sale.

### 5.6 The moat (five, compounding)

1. **Citation network effect** — cited first in the firm's own reports, then by others; once your reference appears in pleadings, it becomes the de facto standard reference.
2. **Proprietary data** — only a working practice produces real measured, adjudicated case evidence; a content mill can't manufacture it.
3. **Maintenance** — keeping every entry current with a moving standards target is continuous labour a late entrant must both catch up on *and* sustain forever.
4. **Completeness** — first to canonically own the finite enumeration means every later resource is permanently "the other one."
5. **Link/authority** — tolerance tables and atlas plates are the natural link target for the whole trade web.

These feed the **instruction flywheel**: cited reference → author credibility rises → more instructions → more casework → more evidence → richer reference — at near-zero marginal cost because the content is a byproduct of billable work.

### 5.7 Structured data (unusually rich — a real advantage here)

`DefinedTermSet`/`DefinedTerm` (glossary) · `TechArticle` per entry (`about`, `citation`, `author`, `reviewedBy`, `dateModified`, `version`) · **`Dataset` for the tolerance tables** (marking the quantitative canon as citable datasets — unusual and powerful) · `HowTo` for method notes · `Person` + `hasCredential` for every author/reviewer · `Organization`/`ProfessionalService` with `publishingPrinciples` + `correctionsPolicy` · `ImageObject` for annotated plates · `citation`/`isBasedOn` linking each entry to the standard it depends on.

### 5.8 Phasing

**Structure-first: an empty-but-rigorous shelf beats a full-but-messy feed.** Build the taxonomy, the identical entry templates, the citation/ID system and the governance rails *before* volume. **Day-one seed** (bounded but complete-*feeling*, at full governance discipline): publish the **Tolerances & Datums tables first** (the single most search- and link-magnetic asset), ~10–15 Defect Atlas entries on the most-litigated failures, 4–6 Standards explainers, 2–3 Method Notes, and a 40–60-term Glossary so the auto-linking graph exists on day one. **Build (18–24 months):** convert each real investigation into atlas + case content toward complete coverage of the defect canon. **Maturation:** contributor bylines as inspectors join; activate the standards-watch cycle; switch on the permanent *"cite this entry"* system.

## 6. The forensic visual system

Seven load-bearing devices turn "premium minimalism" into "evidence record":

1. **The Plate.** Images are never decorative bleeds — each is a *catalogued plate*: image + four corner registration marks + an in-frame photographic scale bar + a plate number (`Plate 04`) + a caption block, sitting inside the grid on hairlines. This one rule converts "stock photo" instinct into "evidence."
2. **The Graticule.** The "grid you can feel" made literal in select places: fine metrology tick marks along a baseline or image edge, with occasional coordinate labels (`grid B/4`). Sparingly — the instrument showing its scale, not graph paper everywhere.
3. **The Calibration Strip.** A recurring, *ownable* device: a small greyscale step-wedge at the foot of plates and as a footer motif. It signals *"this grade is true, nothing here is beautified"* — the anti-Photoshop mark. No competitor will think to use it.
4. **The Specimen Record.** Specimen refs (`SP-07`), project codes (`TCS-2431`), dates, locations and inspector initials as mono microtext under plates — chain-of-custody texture that makes the library read as an archive, not a gallery.
5. **Redaction as a visible device.** The redaction bar is a *designed, honest* element (solid ink bar, optional mono `REDACTED`) — proof the cases are real and that discretion is a service. **Never animate a redaction lifting.**
6. **The Report as Artefact.** Reports treated as hero products two ways: photographed on a copystand as plates, *and* rendered natively as designed exhibit pages with their own typographic furniture (running heads, exhibit numbering, figure captions).
7. **Two registers, rebalanced.** Evidence imagery (macro defect + instrument-in-use) rises to **~80%**; practice imagery (principal portrait + sparse "scene" context) drops to **~20%**, and context shots are reframed as *"the scene of examination"* — desaturated, structural, never estate-agent gloss.

### 6.1 Photography direction

Shoot like **scientific/forensic documentation**, not commercial product photography — the beauty is precision and truth, never drama. Copystand for flat subjects; one consistent macro focal length (~90–100mm equiv); **focus-stacking as standard** (selective-focus bokeh is banned — it hides evidence). The **signature is the lighting**: even diffuse base light *plus* one low-angle **raking light** grazing the surface to throw lippage, crazing and deviation into readable relief (how conservators and crime labs reveal surface), with **cross-polarisation** on glazed/wet tiles to kill glare. **Always in frame:** a physical scale reference, the instrument *in use with its readout legible*, and the greyscale calibration strip. Cool-neutral, slightly desaturated, calibrated-true grade; no filters, no vignette, no crushed blacks. **Compose to be annotated** — subject to one side, generous clean negative space for dimension lines and callouts. *An empty calibrated frame is more premium than a generic stock one — never stock instruments, never a hard-hat handshake.*

### 6.2 A one-day commission (interim reality)

If no library exists yet: **one tight shoot day, ~8–12 plates + one portrait.** Priority setups from the shot list: calliper on a lipped threshold (LCD legible) → homepage hero; 2m straightedge + feeler gauge on a floor → surface-regularity; line laser across a large-format wall; RH probe in a screed with %RH readout; pull-off (dolly) adhesion test; hollowness/percussion survey with chalk-marked debonds; USB-microscope macro of glaze crazing; movement-joint failure; tenting/arched tiles; efflorescence bloom; a redacted report spread on a copystand; the principal at the bench among instruments. (Full 14-item shot list retained in the working notes.)

### 6.3 Annotation grammar (this layer *is* the design)

A single consistent drawing language over every plate. All annotation in the technical **monospace**, tabular-lining, units always explicit to a fixed precision (mm to 0.1, %RH to 0.1, N/mm², L/360). Hairline **extension + dimension lines** (one terminator style, never mixed), engineering **leader lines** ending in a dot on the feature with the label in reserved negative space. **The signal rule:** the instrument-red is reserved for *the measured value that constitutes the finding* and the *out-of-tolerance flag* — nothing else. Canonical tolerance notation:

```
Lippage  2.4 mm   |   permissible ≤ 1 mm (BS 5385-3)   |   NON-CONFORMING
```

Conformance verdict is **text** (`CONFORMING` / `NON-CONFORMING`), never carried by colour alone (colour-blind safe); red may *accompany* the word but never replace it. Consistent `Fig.` / `Plate` / `Exhibit` prefixes (one per context). The discipline *is* the message — a drifting annotation layer reads as amateur.

## 7. Colour, type & motion — the deltas

- **Re-role the red.** It stops being a general "accent/active state" and becomes **strictly metrological**: only (a) the measured value that is the finding and (b) the non-conforming condition. Buttons, links and hovers revert to ink + hairline (they were meant to be quiet anyway). Red becomes rarer and sharper — *when you see it, something was measured and something failed.* This confirms **red over blue** (§8.3, Round 1) and gives it a reason beyond honouring Leica.
- **No traffic-light palette.** Resist a green "pass" — it cheapens and fails colour-blind users. Conformance is stated in words; conforming stays neutral ink.
- **Calibration greys become a formal device** (step-wedge at plate feet / footer). Costs nothing; distinctive.
- **IR/data imagery stays technical** — a single restrained ramp or greyscale, *never* the rainbow jet colormap (reads as a weather app, breaks the grade).
- **The serif is now confirmed IN** (resolving the Round 1 open question). Because reports and the library are hero products, they need authored-document typography — running heads, exhibit numbering, figure captions, standard citations. The three-voice split becomes the primary carrier of the identity given the image-light launch: **grotesque = authority/UI · serif = authored evidence · mono = the instrument.**
- **Motion = measurement gestures only.** The site behaves like an instrument taking a reading, never like a UI performing. Permitted set: *dimension draw* (a dimension line extends to a feature, value resolves in the gap); *value settle* (a number damps to rest like a needle — **not** a hype odometer count-up); *rack into focus* (a macro plate enters slightly soft, racks sharp over ~300ms — "bringing the evidence into focus"); *raking sweep* (the once-or-twice signature — a low light sweeps a hero surface, raising a defect's relief, then settles); *graticule draw*. **Hard prohibitions:** never animate a redaction lifting or a specimen un-blurring (anonymisation must never look reversible); no parallax / Ken Burns over evidence (motion must not distort a measured image); no spring/overshoot anywhere. Reduced-motion default is the *fully-measured, fully-sharp, fully-annotated* plate — arguably the more authoritative state.

## 10. Synthesis critique (standing in for the lost completeness-critic)

Four watch-items the material implies but doesn't fully close:

1. **Forensic ≠ adversarial.** Leaning into evidence risks signalling that instructing the firm *means you're heading to court* — which repels the routine QA / progress / due-diligence buyer who is most of the volume. **Reframe evidence as protection and certainty — the record that means disputes never start — not as a weapon.** Independence *de-risks*; it doesn't escalate. Hold this in the homepage and Sectors copy specifically.
2. **Warm in the margins, exact in the findings.** A pure-instrument brand has no one to put in the witness box, and human buyers read clinical detachment as arrogance. **Quarantine warmth in the human/service layer** — the named inspector shot as an honest portrait, plain courteous language at enquiry and reassurance moments — while the *evidence* layer stays clinical. Clinical + warm-neutral palette = premium; clinical + clinical-white = template.
3. **The naming path is on the critical path.** Because a *citable report archive* and a *cited library* fuse permanently to the mark, the clearance sprint (§1.3) must finish *before* the archive accrues — i.e. before launch content. Don't let brand-naming slip behind build; it gates the two hero products.
4. **Governance is a launch feature, not a Phase-6 nicety.** The library's authority comes from *visible* discipline (version, reviewer, last-reviewed, errata, masthead). Ship the governance rails and the editorial statement *with the first 12 entries*, or the "definitive reference" reads as an ordinary blog and the moat never starts compounding.
